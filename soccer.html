Analyzing the USWNT 2023 World Cup Group Stage

This project focused on analyzing the performance of the United States Women’s National Team during the group stage of the 2023 FIFA Women’s World Cup. The USWNT entered the tournament as the defending champions and were aiming to win a third consecutive World Cup. However, the group stage results were more challenging than expected. The team finished second in Group E after defeating Vietnam 3–0 and drawing with both the Netherlands and Portugal. This was the lowest number of points the team had ever recorded in a World Cup group stage.

To better understand the team’s performance, I worked with match data and analytics metrics commonly used in modern soccer analysis. Instead of looking only at goals scored, the project focused on deeper performance indicators that can better explain how a team actually played during the tournament. Many of the metrics suggested that the United States created strong scoring opportunities but struggled to convert those chances into goals. For example, analytics models estimated that the team should have scored more goals based on the quality of their chances, highlighting a gap between expected goals and actual scoring output.

Using Python and data visualization tools, I cleaned match data, analyzed performance metrics, and created graphics to help explain the story behind the numbers. The goal of the article was to translate complex data into a clear narrative about how the USWNT performed and why the results looked different from previous tournaments.

Key Metrics Analyzed

  • Expected Goals (xG)
  • Expected Assists (xA)
  • Shot Accuracy
  • Attacking Third Touches
  • Expected Goals Against (xGA)

The final analysis was published on American Soccer Analysis, a website focused on soccer data research and analytics. The article combines statistical analysis with visualizations to help explain what the numbers reveal about the team’s performance during the tournament.
